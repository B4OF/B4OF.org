Who can volunteer

You must

  • Be 18 or older (or in a teen-helper role with an adult)
  • Complete the application and interview
  • Pass a criminal background and sex offender registry check
  • Give two references who are not family
  • Complete training and sign the code of conduct

You do not need

  • To be a pro athlete
  • Teaching experience
  • A perfect past (a record is reviewed case by case)
  • To know crypto or finance (we train you)

Section: keeping kids safe

Safety comes first. Always.

Safeguarding is everyone’s job. The next five slides are the rules every volunteer follows, with no exceptions.

Supervision rules

Two-adult rule

At least two screened adults at every session. Never alone with one child out of sight of others.

Ratios

At least one adult for every [ten] kids. Fewer kids per adult for younger ages and boxing drills.

Sign in and out

Kids leave only with a parent or an adult on their form. Check ID if you do not know the person.

Restrooms and changing

Send kids in pairs. Adults do not enter while kids are changing. A second adult waits at the door.

One-on-one talks

Have them in open view, in a room with the door open, and tell the parent beforehand.

Rides

Never drive a child alone. Written parent permission and two adults or multiple kids in the car.

Code of conduct

I will

  • Put kids’ safety and dignity first
  • Be patient, fair and encouraging
  • Stay in open, observable settings
  • Arrive on time, prepared and sober
  • Model good sportsmanship
  • Report any concern right away

I will not

  • Be alone with a child out of sight
  • Use alcohol, tobacco, vapes or drugs with kids
  • Hit, shame, threaten, haze or bully
  • Touch a child in any secretive or unwanted way
  • Give private gifts or favors
  • Carry a weapon at any program

Breaking the code can mean removal from the program and a report to authorities where required.

Texts, social media and photos

Messaging

  • No private texts, DMs or calls with a child
  • Message the parent, or use a group that includes a parent and another adult
  • Keep it about the program

Social media

  • Do not friend or follow minors on personal accounts
  • Do not post kids’ names or locations
  • Use B4OF accounts only

Photos and video

  • Only with written parent permission
  • Only approved staff, on program devices
  • Never in locker rooms or restrooms

If you are worried about a child

1. Danger now: call 911

Then tell the Safeguarding Officer.

2. Suspected abuse or neglect

Call the Illinois DCFS hotline: 1-800-25-ABUSE (1-800-252-2873). You do not need proof.

3. Tell the Safeguarding Officer

[Name, phone, email]. Same day, every time.

4. Write it down

What you saw or heard, the child’s own words, date and time.

Do not investigate, question the child again and again, promise secrets, or tell the person involved. Good-faith reports are always protected.

Coaching safely

Boxing

  • Certified coach leads all contact work
  • Headgear, mouthguard, gloves, protective gear
  • No full-contact sparring for beginners
  • Match by age, weight and experience

Basketball

  • Inspect the court and hoops before play
  • Teach safe landing and ankle care
  • Build up intensity gradually
  • Rotate players and rest often

Heat and hydration

  • Water breaks every [15–20] minutes
  • Shorten sessions in extreme heat
  • Watch for dizziness, nausea, confusion

Head injury: when in doubt, sit them out

  • Stop play at any possible concussion sign
  • Call the parent, call 911 if severe
  • No return until cleared in writing by a medical professional

How to be a great mentor

Show up

Consistency builds trust. Be on time, every time.

Listen first

Ask open questions. Let kids finish. Do not rush to fix.

Praise effort

Name what they worked on, not just the result.

Set high standards

Expect their best and tell them why you believe in them.

Respect privacy

Do not share what a child tells you, except safety concerns you must report.

Know your limits

You are a mentor, not a counselor. Refer big problems to the program lead.

Money lessons you can teach

Ten-minute talks. Use stories and questions, not lectures. We provide the lesson cards.

If something goes wrong

1

Stop and secure

Stop the activity. Keep the group calm and away from the injured child.

2

Get help

First-aid trained adult treats. Call 911 for serious injury.

3

Call the parent

4

Report it

Complete the Incident Report Form the same day.

Know where the first-aid kit, exits, phone and emergency contacts are before every session. Keep a copy of the registration forms with you.

What we expect of you

Be reliable

Arrive on time. If you cannot come, tell the program lead at least [24 hours] ahead so we can cover.

Be prepared

Read the session plan. Bring your ID and a charged phone for emergencies only.

Be professional

Dress for activity, wear your B4OF shirt or badge, and keep your phone away during sessions.

Be a team player

Share concerns with the program lead, not with kids or parents in the gym. Accept feedback.

What you get

More than a good feeling

We take care of the people who take care of our kids.

  • Free training and first-aid or CPR where available
  • Letters of recommendation after [one] season
  • Verified service hours for school or work
  • B4OF shirt and volunteer badge
  • Networking with Chicago leaders and sponsors
  • Recognition at our season showcase

What would you do?

1. A player texts you privately asking for advice.

Answer through the parent or a group chat. Tell the program lead.

2. You are the only adult left and one child’s ride is late.

Call the parent and a second adult. Stay in open view. Never drive them alone.

3. A child says something that worries you about home.

Listen calmly, do not promise secrecy, write it down, report to the Safeguarding Officer the same day. Call DCFS if abuse is suspected.
